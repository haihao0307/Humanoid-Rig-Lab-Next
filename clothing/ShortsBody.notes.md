# ShortsBody source and scope

`ShortsBody` reads the character's own generated `skin` triangles and eight skin influences. Source positions are decoded with the same X reflection, origin and extent as the body renderer. Cloth and body queries use metres. The crop retains the lower torso and thighs, from 60 mm below the source knee pivots to 80 mm above the requested waist, scaled with stature; arm-owned triangles are excluded. `report()` records this range and any original degenerate triangles skipped. It is not an all-body collider.

`measure()` intersects the original triangles. Closed horizontal sections provide waist, upper waistband, hip and separate thigh measurements; the central sagittal section provides the lower front/back rise route. A tensioned planar tape envelope bridges surface grooves. This distinction matters: on the actual generated test body the raw skin route gave 434/481 mm rises, while the external tape route gives 302/380 mm. `metadata.rawSurfacePaths` preserves the raw values. No skin geometry is changed and no skirt silhouette supplies measurements. Hip height is selected from 17 section planes; this is a finite engineering measurement, not a clinical anthropometric certificate.

`update()` reads current committed joint world frames and source bind frames directly, using production DQS and tissue functions; it does not wait for rendering or alter the body. Previous/current surfaces interpolate during cloth substeps. Waist anchors are barycentric points on the original skin triangles. `waistRestSectorPoint(side, front, t, top)` uses `left`/`right`, a front boolean, and `t=0` at centre front/back through `t=1` at the matching side seam. Upper-band height defaults to 28 mm, independently of stature. Only this narrow waistband permits attachment; legs are not skinned to body targets.

An isolated original WFR waistband diagnostic at step 420 was audited separately from whole-short sewing. All 32 standing upper-waist anchors had outward raw geometric normals (minimum cosine with the deformed authored outward field 0.995255), zero uncertain classifications, and at least 2.489822 mm actual global clearance for a requested 2.5 mm offset. Original named-sector points already lay on the skin: nearest-triangle projection changed them by at most 1.16e-16 m. Thus neither inward anchor normals nor snapping a bridged tape groove explained that diagnostic's remaining strain. No attachment or body geometry change was made on this evidence.

Its worst WFR triangle [14,23,22], with bending compliance 40000, had 7.556% principal strain. A strictly local counterexample retained all three original-UV edge lengths exactly and moved points [22,23,14] by only [0.500,2.035,1.006] mm. The two upper points stayed 3.562/3.470 mm from the same actual skin anchors, inside the existing 8 mm fit gap criterion. Static full-triangle distance against the original body BVH was 2.939812 mm, above the 2.5 mm thickness, with no triangle intersection; 1,225 finite samples independently agreed. This rules out a necessary skin-shape obstruction for that single triangle. It does not solve neighboring material, bending, the complete waistband, or garments in motion, and the witness is diagnostic rather than a garment target.

A subsequent external feasibility probe solved the **entire isolated WFR panel**, retaining its 24 vertices, 28 triangles, original UVs and masses. Original-UV metric projections, unilateral real-body contact and an upper-edge support range produced 0.000687% maximum principal strain, 4.492 mm maximum anchor gap and only 2.686 mm maximum motion from the failing snapshot. Full triangle-to-original-skin distances were at least 2.546112 mm across all 28 faces, with no nonadjacent self-intersection. The 32.03984 mm upper edges [17,18] and [21,22] reveal the conflict: their endpoint anchors are each 2.5 mm outside, but the straight chords intersect skin. An additional common outward displacement of 2.870/2.614 mm gives the complete edges 2.5 mm clearance, within the existing 8 mm support allowance. The coarse mesh has a feasible static state; this evidence favors respecting the permitted narrow support range over changing skin or paper. The witness omits bending/gravity energy equilibrium, the other waistband sectors and sewn pants, and is never a production garment target.

Two numerically identical committed DQS/muscle frames have the same surface for every interpolation fraction. During an already stationary interval, `update()` increments its observation count but keeps the geometry version, exact posed triangle cache and valid contact certificates; `sample(alpha)` uses the canonical fraction 1. Motion resumes ordinary interpolation. The first held frame after motion collapses the previous/current interval and invalidates its old certificates. The actual-fixture regression performs 3,456 projections across stationary, moving and newly held frames against the former always-refreshed sampling path; particle positions, nearest points, normals and penetration depths are strictly equal. Stationary body updates measured about 0.25 ms in the local component probe.

Moving frames also reuse an already evaluated previous endpoint only when its geometry version is exactly the preceding version; missed frames recompute the real previous endpoint. Outward normals follow the same rule. Nodes proven outside the authored tissue/shoulder influence support may share a normalized DQS transform only when their ordered joint/weight rows are identical. Scalar accumulation, normalization, dual-quaternion orthogonalization and point arithmetic retain production `r2DeformPoint` order. Tissue-affected and unshared nodes keep that production function. This changes no triangle, nearest-query rule, signed orientation or waist attachment. A direct production-deformation oracle checks moving positions, normals, global queries, projections and all 32 waist anchors, including missed pose versions and alpha 0/0.25/0.5/1.

Two bounded CPU comparisons used the actual skin, 2,760 actual cloth sample positions, ten committed root translations plus small left/right femur/tibia rotations, and no cloth or GPU simulation. Each comparison strictly matched 30,192 closest results and 35,958 node positions/normals. Moving-query median fell from 227.28 to 199.61 ms in one run and 190.66 to 169.18 ms in the second (12.2% and 11.3%). There were occasional slow garbage-collection/runtime samples; these are component gains, not a real-time claim. Full-body signed-distance limitations remain the same as before this performance change.

A later CPU regression sampled eight actual committed controller frames from the recorded walk, turn, sit-down, ground-sit and stand-up sequence, using each selected frame's true adjacent predecessor. At alpha 0/0.5/1, 1,536 closest results, node positions and normals, plus 768 waist attachment positions, strictly matched direct production deformation; 114,072 sampled node/block bound checks passed. This exercised the moving cache on real controller motion rather than invented joint angles. Sixty-one of those arbitrary near-skin query points were explicitly side-uncertain, with up to seven of 64 in a seated pose. Those were retained as unknown, not converted into outside passes. This body-query regression did not simulate cloth or establish garment motion acceptance.

The contact BVH retains every selected source triangle. Spatial blocks use conservative DQS bounds and deform original nodes only when queried. A block bound includes source extent, influence-weight variation, relative joint rotation and translation; ambiguous quaternion hemispheres or possible muscle deformation fall back to exact block bounds. Bounds do not inflate the contact surface. Each query ultimately tests original current triangles. The regression fixture checks every selected source vertex under three poses and three interpolation fractions against the conservative bounds.

Signed distance uses the actual closest feature. Interior face hits use its oriented normal; edge hits use incident face pseudonormals; vertex hits use incident face normals weighted by their current triangle corner angles. Current face orientation is checked against the original outward vertex normals under the same normalized DQS rotation, muscle cofactor and shoulder blend used by rendering. This matters when different bindings flip a tiny source triangle: its geometric cross product can point into the skin even though its authored outward side remains outside. These normals determine orientation only; the true nearest-feature displacement supplies projection distance/direction.

Source vertices within 2 micrometres times stature may share **sign adjacency only**, with equal eight influence weights and tissue deltas and agreeing original normals (dot product above 0.5). This uses representative distances, not transitive chains. Collapsed numerical strips use their adjacent noncollapsed faces for sign. All original positions and all 126,080 selected triangles remain in the actual distance query; no skin position is snapped, discarded, expanded or replaced. The actual fixture merged at most 1.995 micrometres and identified 2,406 such strips. Opposite layers and different bindings have explicit rejection regressions. A query still is not a certified inside/outside predicate for an arbitrary self-intersecting or open skin surface; the report exposes `insideCertificate:false`.

Six actual remote-point counterexamples verify zero attraction. The two additional failures were 141.624 and 64.283 mm outside: one selected a horizontal numerical seam strip, the other a DQS-flipped strip. Independent rays in all four horizontal cardinal directions had even crossing counts. Signed checks at one seam surface plus/minus 1 nm and 1 mm retain distinct inside/outside responses. The recorded step-60 snapshot has 2,760 current cloth samples; after this sign repair its maximum clearance residual is 9.1e-18 m. A separate step-30 profile snapshot also passes all current samples. These are static rechecks, not a rerun or a claim that the old simulation's accumulated strain is repaired.

The later gusset G30/G370 snapshots exposed a further failure: a nearly horizontal source strip, and a vertex dominated by such faces, still had outward-aligned cross products nearly perpendicular to the authored outward field. Flipping the sign cannot repair that direction. Those two actual points were 157.223552 mm and 15.277236 mm outside, yet the former feature sign reported them inside. The repaired path preserves their original closest triangles, closest points and Euclidean distances and produces zero correction at both; neither a skin shell expansion nor an attraction term is used.

Only an ambiguous closest feature invokes a bounded geometric fallback. Ambiguity means a contributing face's signed cosine with its deformed original outward normal is below 0.2 (including every reversed face), or a contributing/pseudonormal is degenerate. This is an orientation reliability criterion, not a change to cloth thickness or contact tolerances. The fallback casts four complete horizontal lines (X, Z and two diagonals) through the original current triangle BVH. It needs at least two independent agreeing directions; both half-lines must have the same parity in every connected skin component. Multiple containing components remain unknown rather than combining layers with XOR parity. No crossing-distance deduplication can erase a real thin layer.

Each line must avoid the posed AABB of every connected open/nonmanifold edge component, including all of its original endpoint copies: a hypothetical closure within that component's convex hull would then not cross the accepted line. It must also avoid every four-endpoint ribbon box between numerical sign-weld edge copies. All 126,080 original triangles remain in the ray and distance queries. Edge, tangent, degenerate, collapsed-strip, coincident or very near surface ray events are rejected for that direction. The actual fixture has 1,751 such guard boxes. This is a finite, conditional side check for the selected procedural surface, not a universal watertightness or arbitrary self-intersecting-solid certificate; `insideCertificate:false` remains unchanged.

When those finite conditions cannot resolve a side, the hit exposes `sideUncertain:true` and `sideEvidence`. Its finite `signedDistance` slot is then only the unsigned distance placeholder, **not** an outside assertion. Projection returns zero depth and leaves the point untouched. Such a hit must never create an empty outside ball. `ShortsSurfaceContact.report()` counts `sideUncertainCount`, retains an example, and fails acceptance even when measured residuals are zero. The independent snapshot audit writes unknown signed distances and penetration quantities as null and also fails sampled acceptance. The production cloth caller discards its outside cache on unknown hits. Regression coverage includes two true sides at ±1 nm / ±1 mm, an open ambiguous plane, distinct-binding nested layers separated by 0.5 micrometres, both actual G failures and unchanged moving-pose/cache equivalence.

`closest(point, alpha)` performs a global nearest-surface query. `project` / `projectPoint` reuse a contact neighbourhood only while the pose, interpolation fraction and displacement certificate remain valid: point displacement plus candidate distance must remain inside the previously queried radius. Crossing that bound triggers a global refresh. `contactConstraint` and `projectConstraint` expose the same mechanism; `projectTriangle` is explicitly local only. Final independent checks should call `closest`. No API provides continuous collision detection, cloth-triangle versus body-surface completeness, or a motion quality certificate. Point contact and local oriented normals require actual garment motion review.

Run `node tools/test-shorts-body.mjs` for small counterexamples. Supplying the external generated-body JSON as a second argument adds actual section, waist-sector, conservative-bound and cached/global nearest-surface checks. The fixture is not a source asset and is not committed. On the development machine the actual fixture retained 126,080 triangles in 2,694 blocks: body update was about 12 ms, 500 first-pass projections about 74 ms, and repeated same-substep projections about 10–15 ms. These are component timings, not an end-to-end frame-rate claim.

Independent finite-sample inspection uses `node tools/audit-shorts-snapshot.mjs BODY_FIXTURE SNAPSHOT OUTPUT_JSON`. The output must be outside this worktree; the external fixture must contain the same committed body pose as the cloth snapshot. Optional flags are `--clearance M`, `--ground-y M`, and `--top N`; otherwise cloth thickness comes from the sibling simulation `source.json`. The tool does not run or correct physics. It checks all cloth vertices, unique edge midpoints and triangle centroids with global actual-skin queries, retaining original UVs and piece/local triangle witnesses. It reports ground minimum separately. A zero sampled failure count is not complete triangle collision clearance. In the recorded old actor-aligned step 420, all 544 vertices passed but 337 interior samples failed clearance, with 15.60 mm maximum skin penetration; independent triangle intersections corroborated this missing face-contact problem.

`ShortsSurfaceContact(particles, triangleRecords, body, {clearanceM, toleranceM, includeVertices})` adds discrete vertex, unique edge-midpoint and triangle-centroid probes to original cloth. `solve(alpha)` recomputes current/previous barycentric points in reusable arrays and distributes a body's unilateral correction as `inverseMass * barycentricWeight / sum(inverseMass * barycentricWeight^2)`. Fixed vertices, original UVs, physical masses and previous positions are preserved. A finite positive exact distance allows reuse of its empty ball while both body pose and interpolation are unchanged. Leaving that ball restores the body query. This is neither attraction nor a maximum permitted skin gap: any outward cloth spacing remains free.

Optional `options.dofs` routes this same actual body correction through completed-stitch spatial degrees of freedom. For measured displacement delta, the scalar constraint is `C = -length(delta)` and each source-corner gradient is `barycentricWeight * delta / length(delta)`. `dofs.project` receives zero compliance/initial lambda and aggregates real gradients and total physical mass, including members belonging to the other sewn panel. The helper does not separately displace corners afterward. A group containing a fixed member remains immovable, and the residual report uses its effective directional inverse mass when counting fixed conflicts. Source UVs, masses and previous positions remain distinct and unchanged. Reusable probe-gradient arrays serve solve calls; reporting remains observational.

The default no-DOF branch is unchanged. A frozen preceding implementation and the adapted module strictly matched every particle, solve result and global report in 150 seeded random/fixed-mass cases across 600 sweeps. Production-DOF integration verifies a completed stitch between mass-1 and mass-3 particles: a midpoint correction moves both sewn members by 8 mm and its independent mass-1 corner by 32 mm, retaining the exact seam and original material identities. Twelve helper tests now include these optional-DOF cases as well as the original contact regressions.

The production stitch-DOF projector reuses internal gradient/correction scratch in synchronous calls instead of allocating a Map and several arrays for each scalar constraint. First-occurrence group order, each floating-point accumulation/multiplication, invalid-input rejection, joined total mass, centre of mass and integration behavior are unchanged. Against the frozen old projector, 180 seeded random/fixed cases matched all particle states, returned lambdas and reports after 44,820 operations, including 5,580 joins and 1,800 integration operations. A bounded benchmark using actual 553-particle/848-triangle data and 90 sewn groups matched complete final states across seven paired runs; median time per 309,700 projections fell from 184.443 to 82.534 ms. This is a 55.25% projector gain, not a whole-frame speed claim. The combined DOF, CCD, surface and current cloth suite passed 73 tests.

`report(alpha)` always uses global closest queries for every current probe and does not modify cloth, probe positions or correction history. It gives `unresolvedCount`, `maxResidualM`, worst original triangle witnesses, fixed conflicts, and per-kind statistics. The default residual tolerance is 1 mm. The solver separately chooses how often to call `solve`. A passed report means only these finite samples pass; `completeTriangleCertificate:false` and `continuousCollisionDetection:false` remain explicit. The helper regressions include outside corners hiding an inside midpoint/centroid on a convex surface, mass distribution, immovable conflicts, cache invalidation and uncertain-side rejection.

The completed-stitch DOF projector also accepts an optional finite `minimumLambda`. It clamps the accumulated multiplier before computing the position increment; a tension-only constraint keeps its existing upper bound of zero. This permits an explicitly configured thread force limit through `minimumLambda = -tensionN * substepSeconds^2`, using the XPBD multiplier/force relation (Macklin, Mueller and Chentanez, XPBD 2016, https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf). It does not select or calibrate a tension value. Omitting the argument preserves the preceding results bit for bit across all 44,820 external random operations. A 1 g + 3 g sewn group pulling an independent 2 g point verifies the cumulative force bound, no extra motion from repeated saturated iterations, release to zero tension, physical centre of mass, and unchanged material identity. The combined DOF/CCD/surface/cloth suite passed 74 tests at this change.
